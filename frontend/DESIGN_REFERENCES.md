# Design Reference Sites — Integra Advisory Partners

The design brief (Section 5) asks for a short list of comparable consulting and
advisory sites to use as a starting reference point. This is that list.

**Purpose:** not to copy, but to agree on a direction — the feel, the density,
and the level of polish the client should expect before work starts.

---

## How to use this list

Each group below represents a different strategic direction for the site. Pick
**one** as the primary reference rather than mixing several.

The current Integra site sits somewhere between groups 2 and 3. That is a
reasonable, safe default — but the brief calls the site "interactive and
intuitive," which argues for a deliberate move rather than staying in the middle.

| Group | Direction | Closest match to the brief |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Editorial and narrative | Best for storytelling and trust |
| 2 | Institutional advisory | Best for credibility and regulatory clarity |
| 3 | Structured conversion | Best for lead capture |
| 4 | Modern and interactive | Best for "interactive" priority |

---

## Group 1 — Editorial and narrative

These sites earn trust through point of view and long-form content rather than
through credentials.

- **Snowball (snowballing.com)** — newsletter-driven advisory site. Very strong
  at making research feel like expertise without over-claiming. Good reference
  for the Insights page.
- **Dense Discovery** — thoughtful, writing-led. Useful for tone, not layout.
- **Longreads or publisher-style sites** generally — useful for the Insights
  and Events pages, where the goal is to be worth reading.

**Why it fits:** the brief's audience includes operators who want real market
conditions, not generic opportunity reports. Point of view is the fastest way to
signal that.

**What to borrow:** article layout, reading rhythm, the way research is presented
as a point of view rather than a service.

---

## Group 2 — Institutional and regulatory

These read as serious advisory firms. Best reference for credibility, legal
guardrails, and the "regulatory clarity" message.

- **Bain & Company** — clear and conservative. Good for the tone of authority.
- **McKinsey** — strong hierarchy and section logic.
- **Brunswick Group** — corporate advisory with a calmer, more modern feel than
  the Big Three.
- **Control Risks** — directly relevant. Crisis and market-intelligence firm
  that handles regulatory and country-risk content well.
- **Averroes / regional law-firm sites for the Gulf** — useful specifically for
  how Doha- and UAE-based firms present authority and local credibility.

**Why it fits:** the disclaimer in the brief ("should not represent itself as a
law firm and should not provide legal advice") means the site needs to project
competence without projecting a legal practice. These sites show how to do that.

**What to borrow:** how to signal authority without over-claiming, and how to
present regulatory content as "navigation" rather than "advice."

**Caution:** this is where the current site already is. Going further in this
direction makes the site feel more corporate and less approachable, which works
against the audience the brief describes.

---

## Group 3 — Structured conversion and advisory packages

These sell defined packages with clear pricing and a clear next step.

- **Stripe** — as a packaging reference. Not for the visual style, but for how
  tiers, comparison tables, and the primary CTA are structured. The current
  Packages page already follows this pattern.
- **Paddle or Lemon Squeezy pricing pages** — clean tier comparison and
  reassurance near the payment step.
- **Notion's marketing site** — good balance of friendly and credible. Closer
  to the tone the brief describes than the Big Three.
- **Linear** — strong use of hierarchy and restraint. Useful for how much to
  show and how little to say.

**Why it fits:** Deliverable 01 specifies five packages with indicative pricing
and a comparison table, and the brief asks for direct online payment.

**What to borrow:** comparison table structure, the reassurance text near the
CTA, and the balance between product information and a single obvious next step.

---

## Group 4 — Modern and interactive

These prioritise motion, scroll behaviour, and an immediate sense of energy.
The brief emphasises "interactive and intuitive... not just a nice-to-have."

- **Framer** — the clearest reference for interactive product marketing.
- **Vercel** — restrained but confident, strong use of space and motion.
- **Arc Browser** — good example of a clear story told visually.
- **Loom** — friendly, approachable, high product feel without losing substance.

**Why it fits:** the brief names interactivity as a priority from Mr. Ahmad
repeatedly. The hero video already exists on the current site.

**What to borrow:** scroll-triggered reveals, restrained motion, the way a video
supports rather than competes with the headline.

**Caution:** this direction pushes toward a "startup" feel, which can undercut
the institutional credibility the firm also needs. Use motion as support, not as
the identity.

---

## Recommendation

**Primary reference: Group 3, with Group 1 for the Insights and Events pages.**

Reasoning:

| Requirement from brief | Group that serves it |
|---|---|
| Interactive and intuitive | Group 4 (motion, restrained) |
| Simplicity over complexity | Group 3 |
| Regulatory clarity, not legal advice | Group 2 (tone only) |
| Lead capture through structured intake | Group 3 |
| Trusted relationship access | Group 1 (point of view) |

This keeps the site credible and conversion-focused while adding the
interactivity the brief asks for, without drifting into a startup aesthetic.

## Secondary question in the brief

The brief asks that design choices appeal specifically to **Black and
African-American audiences in the U.S.**, described as a stated business
requirement rather than a general diversity note.

This is worth settling explicitly before design work continues, because it
affects photography, tone, and reading level far more than a layout reference
will.

Points to decide with the client:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Is the primary reader in the U.S. or in Africa? | The two need different language and imagery |
| Should the firm be presented as a peer ("we have been there") or as an authority ("we know the market")? | Changes every headline and the hero imagery |
| Real photography of clients and partners, or abstract? | Currently the site uses abstract stock |
| Should the U.S. hosting fact be stated on the page? | The brief treats it as a credibility signal |
